# Emotion Identifier Tool: Design Notes

## Experience Direction

The app should feel like an explorable learning tool, not a form. The story page should guide visitors through the emotional and product logic step by step, then let them enter the tool when they are ready.

The tool should remain usable on its own for people who do not want the explanation.

## Story Page

The first version should include:

- A short "why this tool" introduction.
- A quote-style moment about how naming fear makes it easier to face.
- A step-by-step breakdown of the tool journey.
- Screenshot or screenshot-like panels for each stage.
- A clear "Open the tool" button.
- A secondary path for people who want to read the story first.

Future versions can replace placeholder panels with real screenshots, comic art, and custom illustrations.

## Visual Direction

The app should become more visually rich over time:

- Use emojis as quick recognition cues for emotion and need categories.
- Add original comic art throughout the story page and tool.
- Make the experience friendly enough for children and meaningful enough for adults.
- Keep the interface calm, readable, and not overwhelming.

## Navigation Direction

Long lists should be broken into smaller choices.

For needs, show broad categories first. After a user chooses a category, reveal the more specific needs inside that category. This reduces overwhelm and makes the interaction feel more exploratory.

The same pattern may later apply more strongly to emotion selection:

1. Start with good or bad.
2. Choose a broad category.
3. Choose specific words.
4. Connect feelings to needs.
5. Generate and share the reflection.

## Tone

The writing should be direct, human, and lightly personal. It should not over-explain, pathologize, or sound clinical.

The story can acknowledge difficulty while keeping the product hopeful and useful.
