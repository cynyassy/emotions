# Emotion Identifier Tool: Product Context

## Purpose

The Emotion Identifier Tool helps people expand their emotional vocabulary, identify what they are feeling, and share that reflection with others.

The tool intentionally begins with the familiar language many people use when asked how they are: "good" or "bad". From there, it guides people into more nuanced feeling words and then connects those feelings to needs that may be met or unmet.

## Origin Story

When the creator was diagnosed with depression at 23, they did not know how to express what they were experiencing. They only knew something felt "bad" and that they were not feeling "good".

Expressing the experience more clearly first required expanding emotional vocabulary. Mapping emotions to words made it possible to name and identify what was happening in the mind. Acknowledging the emotion also meant admitting it was real.

This tool exists to help people identify and share the emotions they are feeling with others.

## Naming Thesis

An important idea for the story page: if you can name the fear, you can begin to face it.

When emotions are unnamed, they can feel shapeless and overwhelming. Not knowing how to name an emotion can trigger overthinking, and overthinking can intensify the overwhelm.

The tool should communicate that naming an emotion does not magically solve it, but it gives the mind a handle. Once a feeling has a name, it can be noticed, questioned, shared, and worked with.

## Conceptual Foundation

The tool is inspired by Nonviolent Communication (NVC), especially the practice of connecting feelings with needs.

The product is not a clinical or diagnostic tool. It is an educational and reflective experience for self-awareness, emotional vocabulary, and communication.

## Audience

The tool should eventually be usable by both adults and children.

It should be visually rich, approachable, and not feel clinical. Comic art, emojis, illustrations, and staged storytelling can help make the topic easier to enter.

## Content Arc

The public experience should include:

1. Why this tool exists.
2. What the tool does.
3. How to use the tool.
4. The interactive tool itself.
5. A shareable reflection output.

Users should be able to skip the explanation and open the tool directly.

## Current Product Risks

- Long lists of emotions and needs can feel overwhelming.
- The current interface is useful but not yet visually rich enough.
- The story behind the tool is not visible inside the product yet.
- Screenshots and sharing are important because the tool is meant to support communication with others.
